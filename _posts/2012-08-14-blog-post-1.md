Notation
======
$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x) &: \text {Unknown true pdf} \\
p(x) &: \text {Predicted pdf}
\end{aligned}
$$


Score Matching
======

Denoising Score Matching
------
Given the denoising function $$q_{\sigma}(\tilde{x} \mid x)$$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{x} &= x + \epsilon, \epsilon ~ N(0, \sigma ^ 2 I ) \\
q_{\sigma}(\tilde{x}) &= \int q_{\sigma}(\tilde{x} \mid x)q(x)dx \\
\end{aligned}
$$ 

We can define our score function, which is the gradient of the log of our pdf. 
Conceptually, the score points towards the area of density.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s(x) = \frac { \partial log (q_{\sigma}(\partial \tilde{x} \mid x) )}{\partial \tilde{x}}
\end{aligned}
$$ 

Score matching allows us to learn the parameters $$\theta$$ of p(x). Where Z($$\theta$$) is a normalizing constant.



$$
\begin{aligned}
p(x) = \frac{1}{Z(\theta)}\exp(-f(x))\\
\end{aligned}
$$

Our estimated score function is then defined as: 

$$
\begin{aligned}
s_{\theta}(x) &= \frac { \partial log (p_{\theta}(x))}{\partial x}\\
&=\frac { \partial \frac{1}{Z(\theta)}\exp(-f(x)) }{\partial \tilde{x}}\\
&= \frac { \partial log(\exp(-f(x)))}{\partial {x}} - \frac { \partial Z_0}{\partial {x}}\\
&= -\frac { \partial f(x)}{\partial {x}}

\end{aligned}
$$ 

We then train a $$s_{\theta}(x)$$ to approximate the score function and define the objective function using the L2 Norm function:

$$
\begin{aligned}
J(\theta) &= E_{q_{\sigma}(\tilde{x})}[\frac{1}{2} \parallel s_{\theta}(x) - \frac { \partial log (q_{\sigma}(\tilde{x} \mid x) )}{\partial \tilde{x}}\parallel ^ 2 _2]
\end{aligned}
$$ 

We can simplify the partial derivative to obtain

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac { \partial log (q_{\sigma}(\tilde{x} \mid x) )}{\partial \tilde{x}} &= \frac{x-\tilde{x}}{\sigma ^ 2}
\end{aligned}$$



Langevin Dynamics
------
There are two issues with the current samples $$\tilde{x}$$ produced for score matching
1. If the data distribution is supported on a low dimensional manifold—as it is often assumed for many real world datasets—the score will be undefined in the ambient space, and score matching will fail to provide a consistent score estimator. 
2. "The scarcity of training data in low data density regions, e.g., far from the manifold, hinders the 
accuracy of score estimation and slows down the mixing of Langevin dynamics sampling."

To solve this issue we will train a score network to simultaneously estimate scores at 
varying noise levels. Then when sampling, we will start sampling a higher noise level then 
gradually transiton to lower noise levels.

By adding random noise, we ensure that "the resulting distribution does not collapse to a low dimensional manifold."
And by starting with larger noise levels, we ensure samples can be produced in the low density regions.
Finally, by starting with larger noise levels and then annealing to smaller noise levels, we improve the efficiency
of sampling while maintaining accuracy.


Using langevin dynamics, we sample from p(x) using the score function $$s_{\theta}(x)$$.

With a step size $$\delta > 0$$, $$z \sim N(0,1)$$, initial value $$\tilde{x} _0 \sim \pi(x)$$ where $$\pi$$
is a prior

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{x} _t = \tilde{x} _{t-1} + \frac{\delta}{2}\frac{\partial log(p(\tilde{x} _{t-1}))}{\partial x} + \sqrt{\delta}z_t
\end{aligned}
$$ 

As $$\delta \rightarrow 0$$ and T $$\rightarrow \infty$$, $$\tilde{x} _t = p(x)$$.
Thus, if $$s_{\theta}(x) \approx s(x)$$  we can use this method to obtain samples from q(x).

To train our function simultaneously for varying noise levels we use a Noise Conditional Score Network (NCSN). 
Let $$\{\sigma _i\} ^ L _{i=1}$$ be a positive geometric sequence, $$ \frac{\sigma _1}{\sigma _2}=...= \frac{\sigma _{L-1}}{\sigma _L} > 1$$

We aim to train NCSN where $$\forall \sigma \in {\sigma _i} ^ L _{i=1}: s_{\theta}(x, \sigma) \approx \frac { \partial log (q_{\sigma}(x ))}{\partial x}$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
l(\theta; \sigma) &= E_{q_{\sigma}(\tilde{x})}[\frac{1}{2} \parallel s_{\theta}(x) - \frac { \partial log (q_{\sigma}(\tilde{x} \mid x) )}{\partial \tilde{x}}\parallel ^ 2 _2]\\
 &= E_{q_{\sigma}(\tilde{x})}[\frac{1}{2} \parallel s_{\theta}(x) + \frac{\tilde{x}- x}{\sigma ^ 2} \parallel ^ 2 _2]
\end{aligned}
$$ 

We then combine all noise levels to obtain the unified objective

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(\theta; \{ \sigma _i \} ^ L _{i=1}) = \frac {1}{L} \sum_{i=1}^L {\lambda (\sigma _i) l(\theta; \sigma)}
\end{aligned}
$$ 

There are many possible choices of $$\lambda$$. In our case we chose $$\lambda = \sigma _i ^2$$.
We then use the following training algorithm to loop through all noise levels $$\alpha _i$$. Though there
are many possible choices of $$\alpha _i$$, we choose $$\alpha _i = \sigma ^2 _i$$

The resulting samples cover the high dimensional manifold, include regions of low density, 
and produce accurate, and efficient samples from q(x).




Denoising Probability Model
======

Forward Diffusion
------
We chose a tractable distribution as our target prior. Specifically we choose the stanard normal 
distribution $$ \pi(y) = N(0,1) $$. This gives us the resulting markov kernel $$N(x^t ; x^{t-1}\sqrt{(1- \beta ^ t)}, I \beta ^ t)$$

By sequentially multiplying our initial distribution $$q(x^0)$$  by this Markov kernel, we 
will end up with our target distribution $$\pi (y)$$. Thus, we can define each step in our forward 
trajectory by this Markov kernel.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = N(x_t ; x_{t-1} \sqrt{(1-\beta _ t)}, I\beta _ t)
\end{aligned}
$$ 

This noising process continues until $$ x_{t-1} \sqrt{(1-\beta _ t)} $$ is near 0, and 
$$\beta _ t$$ is near 1. The exact value of $$\beta$$ is controlled by a variance schedule

The full forward trajectory is defined by the joint distribution of our timesteps

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x_1, x_2,...,x_t) = q(x_{0...T}) = q(x_0)\prod_{t=1}^T q(x_t \mid x_{t-1})
\end{aligned}
$$ 

In order to efficiently sample when performing forward diffusion, we leverage the reparaterization trick.

This results in the closed form $$x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon$$



Reverse Diffusion
------
We want to know $$q(x_{t-1}│x_t)$$, but the equation is analytically intractable. 
Thus, we instead train $$p(x_{t-1}│x_t)$$  to approximate the function.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(x_{t-1}│x_t) := N(x_{t-1}; \mu _{\theta}(x_t, t), \Sigma _ \theta (x|t, t))\\
p(x_1, x_2,...,x_t) = p(x_{0...T}) = p(x_0)\prod_{t=1}^T p(x_t \mid x_{t-1})
\end{aligned}
$$ 

To initialize $$p_ \theta (x_{t-1} \mid x_t)$$, we set  $$ \Sigma _ \theta (x _t, t) = \sigma _t ^ 2 I$$.  \\
If $$x_0 \sim N(0,1)$$, we set $$ \sigma _t ^ 2 = \beta _t ^ 2 $$   \\
If $$x_0$$ is set to one point, we set $$ \sigma _t ^ 2 = \tilde{\beta} _t ^ 2 = \frac{1- \bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\beta _t $$

Training
------

> **KL Divergence:**  
>KL Divergence measures the expected value of the difference in the log likelihoods
> where p(x) is the true probability distribution and q(x) is the predicted distribution
>  
> $$D_{KL}(q||p) =\int q(x)log(\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}) = E_{x\sim q}[log(\frac{q(x)}{p(x)})]$$
>  

>**Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO):**  
>$$\begin{aligned}
>D_{KL}(q||p) &= \int q(z|x)log(\frac{q(z|x)}{p(z|x)}) \\
>&= \int q(z|x)log({q(z|x)}) - \int q(z|x)log({p(z|x)}) \\
>&= \int q(z|x)log({q(z|x)}) - \int q(z|x)log(\frac{p(z,x)}{p(x)}) \\
>&= \int q(z|x)log({q(z|x)}) - \int q(z|x)log({p(z,x)}) + \int q(z|x)log({p(x)}) \\
>&=  E_{z\sim q}[log({q(z|x)})] - E_{z\sim q}[log({p(z,x)})] + log({p(x)})\int q(z|x) \\
>&=  E_{z\sim q}[log({q(z|x)})] - E_{z\sim q}[log({p(z,x)})] + log({p(x)}) \\
> log({p(x)}) &= D_{KL}(q||p) -  E_{z\sim q}[log({q(z|x)})] + E_{z\sim q}[log({p(z,x)})] \\
>\end{aligned}$$  
>  
> Since KL divergence is always >0
> 
> $$log({p(x)}) \geq -  \underbrace{E_{z\sim q}[log({q(z|x)})] + E_{z\sim q}[log({p(z,x)})]}_{\text{ELBO}}$$
> 
> Since the log likelihood of p will remain constant, by minimizing the 
> right hand terms you indirectly minimize the KL Divergence.


Training optimizes the variational bound on the negative log likelihood by stochastic gradient descent. 

$$
\begin{aligned}
 E_{x\sim q}[log({p(x)})] &\geq -  E_{x\sim q}[log({q(x_{1...T}|x_0)})] + E_{x\sim q}[log({p(x_0,x_{1...T})})]\\   
 E_{x\sim q}[-log({p(x)}]) &\leq  E_{x\sim q}[log({q(x_{1...T}|x_0)})] - E_{x\sim q}[log({p(x_0,x_{1...T})})]\\   
&=  E_{x\sim q}[-log(\frac{p(x_0,x_{1...T})}{q(x_{1...T}|x_0)})]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[-log(\frac{p(x_T)\prod_{t=1}^{T}p(x_{t-1}|x_t)} {\prod_{t=1}^{T}q(x_{t}|x_{t-1})})]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[-log(p(x_T)) -\sum_{t=1}^{T}log(\frac{p(x_{t-1}|x_t)}{q(x_t|x_{t-1})})]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[-log(p(x_T)) -\sum_{t=1}^{T}log(\frac{p(x_{t-1}|x_t)}{q(x_t|x_{t-1})}) - log(\frac{p(x_0|x_1)}{q(x_1|x_0)})]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[-log(p(x_T)) -\sum_{t=2}^{T}log(\frac{p(x_{t-1}|x_t)}{q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)})(\frac{q(x_{t-1}|x_0)}{q(x_t|x_0)}) - log(\frac{p(x_0|x_1)}{q(x_1|x_0)})]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[-log(p(x_T)) -\sum_{t=2}^{T}log(\frac{p(x_{t-1}|x_t)}{q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)}) - \sum_{t=2}^{T}(\frac{q(x_{t-1}|x_0)}{q(x_t|x_0)}) - log(\frac{p(x_0|x_1)}{q(x_1|x_0)})]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[-log(p(x_T)) -\sum_{t=2}^{T}log(\frac{p(x_{t-1}|x_t)}{q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)}) - \sum_{t=2}^{T}log(\frac{q(x_{t-1}|x_0)}{q(x_t|x_0)}) - log(p(x_0|x_1)) + log(q(x_1|x_0))]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[-log(p(x_T)) -\sum_{t=2}^{T}log(\frac{p(x_{t-1}|x_t)}{q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)}) - log(\frac{q(x_1|x_0)}{q(x_T|x_0)}) + log(q(x_1|x_0)) - log(p(x_0|x_1)) ]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[-log(p(x_T)) -\sum_{t=2}^{T}log(\frac{p(x_{t-1}|x_t)}{q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)}) - log(\frac{1}{q(x_T|x_0)}) - log(p(x_0|x_1)) ]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[\frac{-log(p(x_T))}{q(x_T|x_0)} -\sum_{t=2}^{T}log(\frac{p(x_{t-1}|x_t)}{q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)}) - log(p(x_0|x_1)) ]\\
&= E_{x\sim q}{\underbrace{D_{KL}(q(x_T|x_0)||p(x_T))}_{L_T} + \sum_{t=2}^{T}\underbrace{D_{KL}(q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)||p(x_{t-1}|x_t))}_{L_{T-1}} - log(p(x_0|x_1))}
\end{aligned}
$$


> **Gaussian Distribution:**  
> Let $$x \sim N(\mu, \sigma)$$. We can then define the pdf as:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> f(x) &= \frac{exp(-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{x- \mu}{\sigma})^2)}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}}\\
> & \propto exp(-\frac{(x- \mu) ^2}{2\sigma ^2})\\
> &= exp(-\frac{1}{2} \frac{(x^2 -2 \mu x + \mu ^ 2)}{\sigma ^2})
>\end{aligned}$$  
>


To continue, we must first define $$q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0)$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0) &= q(x_t | x_{t-1}, x_0)\frac{q(x_t-1 | x_0)}{q(x_t | x_0)}\\
&= \frac{N(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}}x_0, (1-\alpha _t)I) - N(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1} x_0, (1-\bar{\alpha}_t-1)I})}{N(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0, (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)I)}\\
&\propto \exp[-\frac{1}{2}[\frac{(x_t - \sqrt {\alpha _t}x_{t-1})^2}{(1-\alpha _t)} + \frac{(x_{t-1} - \sqrt {\bar{\alpha} _{t-1}}x_0)^2}{(1-\bar{\alpha} _{t-1})}] - \frac{(x_{t-1} - \sqrt {\alpha _t} x_0)^2}{(1-\bar{\alpha} _t)}]\\
&= \exp[-\frac{1}{2}[\frac{(x_t - \sqrt {\alpha _t}x_{t-1})^2}{(\beta _t)} + \frac{(x_{t-1} - \sqrt {\bar{\alpha} _{t-1}}x_0)^2}{(1-\bar{\alpha} _{t-1})}] - \frac{(x_{t-1} - \sqrt {\alpha _t} x_0)^2}{(1-\bar{\alpha} _t)}]\\

&= \exp[-\frac{1}{2}[\frac{x_t^2 - 2\sqrt {\alpha _t}x_{t-1}x_t + \alpha _t x_{t-1}^2}{\beta _t} + \frac{x_{t-1}^2 - 2\sqrt {\bar{\alpha} _{t-1}}x_0 x_{t-1} + \bar{\alpha}_{t-1} x_0 ^2}{(1-\bar{\alpha} _{t-1})} - \frac{(x_{t-1} - \sqrt {\alpha _t} x_0)^2}{(1-\bar{\alpha} _t)}]]\\

&= \exp [-\frac{1}{2}[(\frac{\alpha _t}{\beta _t} + \frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}) x^2 _{t-1} - (\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha _t} x_t}{\beta _t} + \frac{2\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}x_0}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})x_{t-1} + \frac{x^2}{\beta _t} + \frac{\bar {\alpha}_{t-1} x^2 _0}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}} - \frac{(x_t-1 - \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0)^2}{1-\bar{\alpha}_t} ]]\\

&= \exp [-\frac{1}{2}[(\frac{\alpha _t}{\beta _t} + \frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}) x^2 _{t-1} - (\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha _t} x_t}{\beta _t} + \frac{2\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}x_0}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})x_{t-1} + C(x_t, x_0) ]]\\

&\propto \exp [-\frac{1}{2}[(\frac{\alpha _t}{\beta _t} + \frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}) x^2 _{t-1} - (\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha _t} x_t}{\beta _t} + \frac{2\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}x_0}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})x_{t-1}]]
\end{aligned}
$$


Since our collection of terms $$C(x_t, x_0)$$ does not contain $$x_{t-1}$$, we can treat it as a constant and ignore it. 

Now, to rewrite this as a normal distribution, we must identify the mean and variance of our distribution. According to the Normal PDF described earlier, we expect out $$x^2$$ term to have a coefficient of 1 and our x term to have a coefficient of $$-2\mu$$. 

To satifsy this constrain, we can define our variance as:

$$\tilde{\beta}_t = (\frac{\alpha _t}{\beta _t} + \frac {1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})^{-1}$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0) &= \exp [-\frac{1}{2}[x^2 _{t-1} - (\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha _t} x_t}{\beta _t} + \frac{2\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}x_0}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})x_{t-1}]]\\
&= \exp [-\frac{1}{2}[\frac{x^2 _{t-1} - (\frac{\alpha _t}{\beta _t} + \frac {1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})^{-1}(\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha _t} x_t}{\beta _t} + \frac{2\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}x_0}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})x_{t-1}}{(\frac{\alpha _t}{\beta _t} + \frac {1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})^{-1}}]]
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now define our $$\mu$$ as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{\mu}(x_t, x_0) &= (\frac{\alpha _t}{\beta _t} + \frac {1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})^{-1}(\frac{x_t \sqrt{\alpha _t}}{\beta _t} + \frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}x_0}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})\\
&= \frac{\sqrt{\alpha}_t(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1})x_t + \beta _t\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}x_0}}{\alpha _t - \alpha _t \bar{\alpha}_{t-1} + \beta _t}\\
&= \frac{\sqrt{\alpha _t} (1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1})}{1 -\bar{\alpha}_t}x_t + \frac{\beta _t \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the parameterization $$x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon$$, we can substitue for $$x_0$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{\mu} &= \frac{\sqrt{\alpha _t} (1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1})}{1 -\bar{\alpha}_t}x_t + \frac{\beta _t \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\frac{x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon _t}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}}\\
&= \frac{\sqrt{\alpha _t} (1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1})}{1 -\bar{\alpha}_t}x_t + \frac{\beta _t}{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\frac{x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon _t}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\\
&= \frac{(\alpha _t(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}) + \beta _t)x_t - \beta _t \sqrt{1- \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon _t}{\sqrt{\alpha _t}(1- \bar{\alpha _t})}\\
&= \frac{(1- \bar{\alpha}_t) x_t - \beta _t \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon _t}{\sqrt{\alpha _t}(1-\bar{\alpha} _t)}\\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha _t}}(x_t - \frac{\beta _t}{\sqrt{1- \bar{\alpha} _t}}) \epsilon _t
\end{aligned}
$$

If we set $$\beta _t$$ as fixed, we can ignore the $$L_T$$ term when training. We choose to reparameterize 
$$L_{t-1}$$ in terms of the mean $$\mu _\theta (x_t, t)$$ by using the following proof (INSERT LINK).

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{t-1} &=  E_{x\sim q}[D_{KL}(q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)||p(x_{t-1}|x_t))]\\
&=  E_{x\sim q}[\frac{1}{2 \sigma _t ^2} \parallel \tilde{\mu}_t (x_t, x_0) - \mu_{\theta} (x_t, x_t) \parallel ^2 ] + C\\


\end{aligned}
$$

where C is a constant that does not depend on $$\theta$$. We can the rewrite the equation in terms of 
$$x_t(x_0, \epsilon) = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon$$ for $$\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{t-1} - C &=  E_{x\sim q}[\frac{1}{2 \sigma _t ^2} \parallel \tilde{\mu}_t (x_t, x_0) - \mu_{\theta} (x_t, t) \parallel ^2 ]\\
L_{t-1} - C &=  E_{x\sim q}[\frac{1}{2 \sigma _t ^2} \parallel \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t - \frac{\beta _t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\epsilon) - \mu_{\theta} (x_t, t) \parallel ^2] \\
\end{aligned}
$$

"Thus, $$\mu _{\theta}$$ must predict $$ \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t - \frac{\beta _t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\epsilon) - \mu_{\theta} (x_t, t)$$ given x_t. Since $$x_t$$ is available as input to the model, we may choose the parameterization"

$$ \mu _{\theta} (x_t, t) =\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t - \frac{\beta _t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\epsilon _{\theta}(x_t, t))$$

"Where $$\epsilon _{\theta}$$ is a function approximator intended to predict $$\epsilon $$ from $$x_t$$."
"To sample $$ x_{t-1} \sim p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t)$$ is to compute

$$x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha _t}}(x_t - \frac{\beta _t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}})\epsilon _{\theta} (x_t, t) + \sigma _t z $$

where $$z \sim N(0, I)$$

The complete sampling procedure resembles Langevin dynamics with $$\epsilon _{\theta}$$ as a learned gradient of the data density. We can further simplify our objective function to


$$
\begin{aligned}

L_{t-1} - C &=  E_{x\sim q}[\frac{1}{2 \sigma _t ^2} \parallel \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t - \frac{\beta _t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\epsilon) - \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha _t}}(x_t - \frac{\beta _t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}})\epsilon _{\theta} (x_t, t)] \\
\end{aligned}
$$
