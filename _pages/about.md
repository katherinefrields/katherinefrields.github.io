The universe is a machine, and every scientific discipline--from physics to chemistry to the depths of mathematics--offers us a glimpse into its near-infinite complexity. Moreover, between these fields lie endless myriads of interactions, and in no other field is this more true than Climate Data Science. 

Through the convergence of machine learning and climate science, I strive to advance our understanding of our planet and our future. Given the growing effects of climate change on human populations, accurate and applicable climate projections are more critical than ever before. Furthermore, with nearly annual groundbreaking advancements in AI and the ever climbing accuracy of AI Climate models, vast opportunities have opened up for rapid advancement in climate projections and scientific knowledge.

By integrating physics with machine learning, these models have achieved stable long-term projections at a fraction of the computational cost, opening the door to finer-scale resolution of atmospheric and ocean dynamics. Within the training of these models lies further potential for data assmiliation, uncertainty quantification, as well as the use of stochastic parameterization to improve model accuracy.

My own work in machine learning applications to climate and earth science has spanned topics from microphysics to global dynamics. During my time as a Data Science and Earth Science major at UCI, I have had the opportunity to work on uncertainty quantification for hybrid physics-ML climate models in the ClimSim Online framework. My research demonstrated the potential of confidence loss as an accurate and low-cost method of uncertainty quantification. Additionally, this past summer, I had the opportunity to conduct research as part of LEAP, an NSF science and technology center dedicated to Learning the Earth with Artificial Intelligence and Physics. There, I worked on developing a diffusion model to generate thermodynamic back-trajectories for ice crystal images taken from cirrus clouds.

These experiences have further encouraged me to pursue a PhD at the intersection of machine learning and climate science. I am honored to be part of this global scientific effort, and I am excited to continue my research in this growing field.
